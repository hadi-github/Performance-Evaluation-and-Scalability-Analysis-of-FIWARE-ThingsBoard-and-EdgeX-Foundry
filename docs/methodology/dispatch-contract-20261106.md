## Primary and secondary endpoints

**Execution-status note (2026-11-07):** the prospective policy below was frozen
for the exact ordinary matrix and received conditional independent release.
"Review candidate" and "requiring review" wording below records the proposal's
original context; it is not permission to retune thresholds. Current execution,
qualification caveats and unresolved attempts are in [status](../status.md).

For each planned message identity, join its complete request record to its earliest complete-message sink receipt:

`delivery_delay_ms = receipt_complete_body_read_ms - actual_send_ms`

`actual_send_ms` is sampled on the **load host immediately before application invocation of the HTTP request, after payload preparation**. Call it **application dispatch**, not network-interface transmission or server acceptance. The Go sink's timestamp is sampled on the **same load host**, after bounded complete body read and before parsing. Retain both host identities, timestamp provenance and sink completeness counters; cross-host component latency remains out of scope.

The dispatch clock is JavaScript `Date.now()`, epoch milliseconds with **1 ms representation resolution**; the sink uses Go wall-clock epoch milliseconds from a nanosecond timestamp. Linux clock API resolution and empirically observed increments are recorded separately; neither nanosecond API resolution nor floating-point fractional milliseconds imply nanosecond dispatch accuracy. At contemporary epoch values, float64 receipt milliseconds have roughly 0.000244 ms spacing. Report actual observed quantization, sampling overhead limitations, and no sub-millisecond dispatch precision claim.

Retain realtime/monotonic bracket sampling on the load host through workload and receipt observation. Missing clock coverage, an unresolved step-discontinuity candidate, or a negative joined delay makes latency **not assessed/invalid measurement**, not negative platform latency. A sampling check cannot exclude an undetected transient clock step between samples. Keep receipt completeness and clock validity separate from successful delivery.

Primary per-run measurement-phase p50/p95/p99 use individual first-receipt dispatch delays, **nearest-rank**, conditional on receipt by the stated cutoff. Never subtract aggregate percentiles. Publish the numerator, scheduled/dispatched cohort denominators and missing fraction next to conditional p99. Zero received means null latency, not zero latency. Warm-up is assessed and reported separately; it is not removed because it looks unfavorable.

Retain scheduled-to-complete-body-receipt per-message distributions as secondary wherever identity/clock evidence supports them. Scheduled timestamps, application dispatch lag, HTTP response latency and elapsed preparation intervals remain workload diagnostics. The former universal send-lag p99 ≤10 ms rule is **withdrawn prospectively**, not relaxed retrospectively. A send-lag value above 10 ms alone is no longer invalidity under this policy.

## Frozen prospective workload-integrity policy (review candidate)

Apply the following to both phases before interpreting platform outcomes. Parameters are fixed before new campaign outcomes; changes require a new policy/version, never a retrospective exception.

1. **Accounting and open loop:** preserve constant-arrival open-loop k6 executors, zero dropped iterations, exact planned identity coverage, one complete dispatch/HTTP-outcome row per planned message, no duplicate dispatches or unexpected identities, and ≥99% achieved offered rate. Count attempted requests, not successful acknowledgements, as offered load. Slow responses, UNKNOWNs and platform errors must not reduce the schedule silently. Missing request records are measurement invalidity, not delivery loss.
2. **Dispatch distribution:** retain actual dispatch counts in non-overlapping half-open `[start,end)` 1 s and 100 ms bins, anchored at each scheduled phase start, with end-spill explicitly reported. Offered rate is actual dispatches physically inside that scheduled phase window divided by its duration, not the eventual scheduled-cohort count. Physical bins include dispatches from *all* message cohorts, including any warm-up spill into measurement; separately publish cohort and spill counts. For the frozen ordinary 25/s cell, require each fully covered 1 s bin to have **23–27 dispatches** (±2 requests), and each fully covered 100 ms bin to have **at most 7 dispatches** (twice rounded-up planned occupancy plus one). These are prospective tolerances for approximately constant offered load, not real-time deadlines. Retain all messages and disclose excursions; a violation invalidates the workload, not the platform. These tolerances permit modest jitter, but not catch-up trains that materially substitute a different arrival process.
3. **Gaps and lag growth:** maximum adjacent physical dispatch gap within a scheduled phase window must be **≤5 scheduled periods** (200 ms at 25/s). Include phase-edge-to-first/last-dispatch gaps in the assessment. Compare median send lag in the final versus initial 10% of the phase; phase and decile membership use **scheduled identity/order**, never observed dispatch order, and growth must be **≤1 scheduled period** (40 ms at 25/s). Exact planned identity accounting extends through the declared collection cutoff; dispatches outside their scheduled phase are retained as spill and do not count toward that phase's achieved physical rate. Report per-minute and phase-wide lag p50/p99/max even when all criteria pass. Fixed latency offsets alone are not overload; growing backlog in the generator or long gaps/bursts change offered-load interpretation.
4. **Saturation:** retain per-core utilization, generator memory, cgroup throttling, VUs and sampler coverage. Zero drops is mandatory. Any OOM/restart, active swapping attributable to the generator, or missing resource evidence leaves generator validity unqualified. A ≥95% busy 10 s interval is a saturation flag; if accompanied by distribution, gap, lag-growth or offered-rate failure, mark workload invalid. High utilization by itself is not proof of platform capacity or measurement failure. Reuse the adopted **≥2× generator/receiver offered-load headroom** evidence where actual adapter, recording and configuration match; do not claim an untested rate is qualified.
5. **Calibrated configuration:** retain k6 1.8.1, generator CPUs 0–1, sink CPU 2, monitoring CPU 3, keep-alive, fixed VU allocation and **`GOGC=400`**, as documented in prior calibration (`generator-status.md`, `calibration.md`). Record actual environment and memory use. Recent diagnostic runs omitted that calibrated setting; this does not establish GC as their sole cause. Required remote-write monitoring remains enabled in final runs.

The new distribution/gap/trend tolerances are concrete **prospective recommendations requiring Sol's freeze review**, not previously adopted criteria. Their scientific effect is to restrict conclusions to approximately constant offered load; they do not certify exact schedule execution or erase timing jitter. They must not be tuned after campaign results.

**Headroom check:** qualify the 25/s cell with one bounded end-to-end run at **50/s** under the same adapter, recording, VUs, generator allocation, calibrated environment and complete-body sink. Its prespecified limits are 48–52 dispatches per complete 1 s bin, ≤11 per complete 100 ms bin, ≤100 ms physical gaps (five periods), and ≤20 ms final-minus-initial decile median lag growth (one period). Achieved physical rate remains ≥99%, complete records and zero dropped iterations are mandatory. A 30 s warm-up plus 120 s measurement bounds this qualification; it is not a campaign replicate. Reuse prior receiver capacity evidence only with its semantic/configuration limitations disclosed. Platform delivery failures during qualification do not automatically establish generator failure; independently validate endpoint joins and workload integrity.

**Narrowed ordinary scope:** this phase deliberately omits the full capacity sweep and freezes 25/s during both the adopted 300 s warm-up and 600 s measurement, rather than deriving warm-up rate from an unavailable prior capacity estimate. This is a prospective bounded-configuration comparison, not execution of the full D39 capacity protocol. Retain five repetitions per platform; disclose reused deployment/database state and do not claim cold-start IID replications. Platform blocks are interleaved across repetitions in the frozen execution matrix rather than letting one platform monopolize one time-of-day block.

## Delivery, outcomes and verdicts

Retain every HTTP outcome including UNKNOWN, incomplete and explicit failure, with original status/error. Reconcile planned/request, sink and history identities separately. Publish missing-by-cutoff, receipts after cutoff, extra duplicate receipts, unexpected identities, acknowledged-but-history-absent and UNKNOWN-but-delivered counts. A first receipt after cutoff is late and remains missing-by-cutoff; never rewrite it as on-time. Never infer a receipt from a log, ACK, later sequence or history record.

Preserve the existing declared 900 s drain ceiling and separate 30 s quiet/30 s late-observation attestation for this scope. The quiet interval is an **observed platform drain outcome**, not a prerequisite for the integrity of an otherwise healthy full-window capture; ongoing delivery at the ceiling remains a platform result, with right-censoring/late counts reported. Likewise, generator invalidity must not be hidden by declaring its otherwise intact sink recording incomplete: keep workload and capture assessments separate. A complete healthy capture with missing delivery or an explicit platform error is a **valid platform failure/result**, not automatically a measurement invalidity. Capture loss, sink overflow, a broken/incompatible parser, incompatible clocks or incomplete request accounting are **measurement invalidity**. Malformed/truncated output from the platform under a healthy capture is instead a potentially valid platform correctness/delivery failure; preserve the body/error evidence and state when identity-specific latency is unavailable. Unparsed/incomplete-body counters require attribution before assigning invalidity—they must not automatically exclude unfavorable platform behavior. Report available conditional latency with its limitations rather than letting missing identities disappear from the interpretation.

## Paper implications and exclusions

This amendment replaces scheduled-to-receipt as the primary endpoint for future campaign runs. Dispatch-to-receipt measures the platform delivery path after the application has invoked the request, excluding pre-dispatch scheduling/preparation delay. It **cannot establish real-world scheduled-event timeliness** or exact wire-to-wire latency. Any real-timeliness claim must use the retained secondary scheduled-to-receipt evidence plus workload integrity and missing-message accounting, not the primary p99 alone. Historical and amended metrics are not pooled without explicit relabeling/reanalysis of raw compatible records.

No hardware upgrade, replacement generator, maximum-capacity search, full-scale scalability claim or automated fault injection is authorized by this amendment. Manual replica-stop experiments remain separate operator-controlled batches. A bounded ordinary-workload result, including a valid platform failure, remains publishable within its explicitly tested configuration.
