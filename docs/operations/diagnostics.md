## Step 1: exploratory verdicts under D23 (goodput at persistence)

EdgeX outstanding work at persistence = scheduled ≤ t minus stored ≤ t, with stored(t) from the raw scraped samples of `pg_stat_user_tables_n_tup_ins{relname="reading"}` (`scripts/persist-series.sh`; the per-id store times were lost with the databases at the next cold start). Stored = counter − its value at the warm-up start, linear between samples; outstanding work is the exact time-average over the last 10 s of each minute (`benchctl explore -persist`). The unchanged criterion is applied; `verdict_persistence` is in each run's `explore.json`.

| Run | Sink verdict: highest pass / first fail | Persistence verdict (D23) | Persistence outstanding min 1/2/3 at first fail | Limit (min 1 + rate × 1 s) | Series resolution | Stored at last point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EdgeX k4 | 700 / 1000 (INVALID) | **350 / 500** (FAIL) | 5,717.6 / 11,750.5 / 17,463.7 | 5,717.6 + 500 | 427 pts, 5 s | 527,971 |

Your expectation is confirmed: k = 4 fails between 350 and 500; k = 1 fails at 200. Stored at the last point vs the history export: k = 4 527,971 vs 528,000 (-29), k = 1 214,954 vs 214,812 (+142): the baseline is interpolated at the warm-up start, so readings already flowing (k = 4) or gate readings still being written (k = 1) shift it by a constant, which cancels in the min 3 − min 1 criterion. Resolution: 5 s scrapes plus Postgres's statistics flush (≤ 1 s for busy backends). Margin, as growth (min 3 − min 1) ÷ slack (rate × 1 s): passing steps at most 0.22 (k4) / 0.46 (k1), failing steps 23.5 / 37.9; no verdict is close to the threshold of 1.

**Decision section of `docs/methodology/exploratory-capacity.md`:** the declared-rule result is unchanged, and D23/D24 now apply: no platform passes 500 msg/s at persistence (EdgeX k = 4 highest pass 350, ThingsBoard 200, FIWARE 100), so D24's reopen condition (tuned capacity > 450) is not met by the exploratory data. That doc is updated in this commit.

**Do the other sinks observe after persistence?**

- ThingsBoard: yes. Orion's write concern is *acknowledged* = applied in memory by MongoDB, not journaled (perf_tuning.md, Write concern), and Orion keeps only the latest value (no history; D9).

Method: the regression approach in Spinner, Casale, Brosig, Kounev, *Evaluating approaches to resource demand estimation*, Performance Evaluation 92 (2015). The utilization-law columns stay in `docs/methodology/exploratory-capacity.md` (labelled).
