## Correction

The scale parser previously compared scientific-notation exponents to coefficient length. That wrongly rejected valid typed timestamps formatted by Go as `1e+06` or `1.7e+12`, while JavaScript accepted them. The coefficient-length guard is removed. Exponent parsing remains bounded to ±10,000, zero is still determined exactly from coefficient digits, and Go's scale subtraction saturates at integer bounds rather than overflowing. The resulting effective decimal scale is `fraction_digits - trailing_coefficient_zeroes - exponent`; valid large powers of ten have nonpositive scale. No float-underflow-to-zero path was introduced. The raw stored-token canonicalization and nonzero-underflow rejection remain unchanged.

No protocol definition or amendment text changed: the established timestamp acceptance contract is unchanged. Protocol JSON SHA-256 remains `7176b1faed9f5d1b08720f8c2e38256418c4cef65b1182e6157c9b276d0d24ee`; D44 profile SHA-256 remains `1b6aa76d5156eeb1561b0d02641b6f01d3ca6a3447904182a3c219b5930ed1f8`.

## Timestamp coverage

Go and JS tests now cover zero and one, every power of ten from `10^6` through `10^15` plus its immediate integer neighbors, Unix-millisecond examples `1700000000000` and `1793664000000`, safe-integer maximum and its predecessor, and three-decimal values at `1000000.001`, `1700000000000.001`, and `1793664000000.001`.
